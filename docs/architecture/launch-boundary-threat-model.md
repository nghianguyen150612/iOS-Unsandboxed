# Launch-boundary threat model

An attacker may replace a pathname, modify an inode in place, introduce a symlink or hard link, change owner/mode/size/link count, supply malformed or stale evidence, rename a protected executable, or copy it after scope enrollment. P012 compares descriptor and path identities and hashes the opened bytes. It rejects unsafe descriptor types, hard links and symlink traversal, detects observed replacements and mutations, and refuses unknown origin. A structural CodeDirectory is not a signature verification. Protected P003 paths and authenticated protected fixture assertions are negative decisions; the fixture is not a comprehensive protected-file catalog.

Checks are snapshots. A file, scope, policy or evidence may change immediately after the last check; pathname-based execution can consume a different object. Concurrent modification, filesystem timestamp semantics, inode reuse, and evidence collection outside this API require additional design. The caller must supply fresh evidence to revalidation; a malicious caller can replay bytes, and P012 never treats that as an authorization. Real execution requires a reviewed exact-object launch boundary with independently authenticated origin, policy and runtime state, and a fail-closed recovery path. P012 neither prepares nor executes the artifact and makes no claim of device behavior.
